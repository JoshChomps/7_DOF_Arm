So **J2 goes first**, not C34 as [22](22_print_readiness.md) §6 planned on 25 Oct. Its module is the real version of
the bench gearbox on the no-parts list, and it carries the largest load in the arm.

Everything below was generated by `tools/coupons.py` and `tools/print_pack.py`, and sliced with the Bambu Studio CLI
on the A1 mini profile in PETG HF (6 walls, 40% gyroid, tree supports; the settings of `tools/slice_parts.py`). The
`.3mf` files open in Bambu Studio with the parts placed and the settings loaded. Re-slice there before printing, and
check the supports in the preview. Nothing here has been printed yet.

The coupons now match the CAD. Before today they tested 623/608 seats and 3/8 mm pegs, and they had no column at the
CAD's +0.05 mm pin fit or its 6.4 mm M5 pocket. Each coupon now has a column at the current guess, plus columns on
either side of it:

| Coupon | Rows | Offsets (mm) | CAD now |
|---|---|---|---|
| pins | Ø3, 4, 5, 6 | −0.05 … +0.20 | +0.05 sliding (ring pins), 0 press (output pins) |
| bearings | 623 (10), 6802 (24), 6804 (32) | 0 … +0.15 | +0.05 |
| pegs | 6802 (15), 6804 (20) journals | −0.15 … 0 | −0.05 |
| inserts | M3 4.2, M4 5.6, M5 6.4 | −0.2 … +0.2 | as the row |

The ring coupons print as eight separate pieces: four 4 mm seat rings, each labelled on a tab, and four journal cups,
each labelled on its floor. Press a real bearing into the rings and onto the cups.

**The C2 housing fits the A1 mini.** `slice.json` lists it on the CORE One L, but only because the slicer CLI's
auto-arrange keeps a 24 mm brim margin round each part and so rejects anything wider than about 130 mm. At
144 × 132.5 mm it slices on the A1 mini in 12.2 h with the input shaft on the same plate. `print_pack.py` places the
parts itself to get round this.

## 3. Assembly (output side first)

Positions are in the module frame of [20](20_cad_full_arm.md) §2: z 0 is the input face, output towards +z.

1. **Inserts.** Melt in the 5 cover inserts (housing, input face), the 4 carrier inserts (flange stem end), the 6 link
   inserts (flange top), the 3 stand inserts and the pulley insert (§5).
2. **Output pins.** Press the 8 Ø6 × 25 pins into the carrier's pockets from the disc side, square (press fit, 0).
3. **Output bearings.** Lower 6810 into the housing from the disc side, up against the land. Drop the inner spacer
   (Ø54/50) in from the output side, then press the upper 6810 into its seat from the output side.
4. **Output stack.** Slide the flange stem down through the upper bearing, the spacer and the lower bearing. Fit the
   carrier from below onto the stem end and pull it up with the 4 M4 × 16 through its counterbores. Tighten them
   evenly: the 0.2 mm gap between the stem and the carrier lets the screws clamp the two inner rings and the spacer
   together. The output should still turn freely by hand. If it binds, the 6810 seats are too tight (the ring coupon).
5. **Ring pins.** With the housing output side down, drop the 25 Ø6 × 30 pins into their holes from the input face.
6. **Input shaft.** Slide one 6804 onto lobe A from the long (input) end, and the other onto lobe B from the short end;
   the two dividers keep them apart. Fit the second 6802 on the short end's Ø15 journal. Put disc A on the lobe A
   bearing and disc B on the lobe B bearing. They are different parts: disc B's outline is turned half a lobe.
7. **Into the housing.** Lower the shaft, short end first: the 6802 enters the carrier's seat, the output pins enter
   the disc holes, and the discs settle between the ring pins. Turn the shaft slowly until everything drops in.
8. **Cover.** Press the first 6802 into the cover from the inside, up against the lip. Lower the cover over the shaft
   and onto the ring pins (and the dowels if fitted). Screw down with 5 M4 × 35.
9. **Pulley.** Slide the 40T pulley onto the shaft end with its key on the flat, until its hub touches the 6802's inner
   ring. Tighten the grub screw onto the flat. The pulley hub and the shaft collar clamp that inner ring between them,
   and this is what holds the input shaft in place along its axis.
10. **Bench.** Screw the stand to the bench and the module's three tabs onto its posts (3 M4 × 16), output up. Bolt the
    lever to the flange (6 M4 × 12).

The encoder cap and the motor plate wait: the cap's posts bolt through the plate, and the cap holds an MR126 and
board C, which have not been bought or made yet.

## 4. Tests with no motor

The full procedure, with the motor holding the input and the MT6701 reading the output, is
[tests/actuator_bench.md](../tests/actuator_bench.md). These are the parts of it that work by hand now. Put the
results in that file and mark them "hand, no motor".

| Test | How | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio and smoothness | Mark the pulley and the lever. 24 pulley turns should be one output turn. Feel for tight spots: one per pulley turn means an eccentric or disc problem, one per output turn means the output stack | The cycloid works; where the friction comes from |
| Input friction | Wind a string round the pulley's teeth (pitch radius 19.1 mm) and pull with a luggage scale until it turns. Torque = force × 0.0191 m | Unloaded drag. The motor sees half of it, through the 2:1 belt |
| Backlash | Clamp the pulley (locking pliers on its flange, braced on a stand post). Push the lever tip lightly each way and read its travel on paper taped under the tip at R 105 mm. Backlash ≈ travel / 105 rad (1 mm ≈ 0.55°) | Rough, because the clamp flexes. Compare with the 0.5° in `arm.toml` |
| Stiffness | Same clamp. Hang 1, 2 and 3 kg from the hook hole (R 88 mm: 1 kg = 0.86 N·m) and read the tip | Rough k. The motor-held push test replaces it |
| Back-driving | Pulley free. Find the smallest weight at the hook that turns the output | Whether J2 holds the arm unpowered after an E-stop |
| Mass | Weigh the module without the motor | Compare with 1.01 + 0.22 kg in `arm.toml` (CAD) |

The lever is 105 mm, not the 200 mm the bench file suggests, because it has to fit the A1 mini's bed. The bench
procedure's numbers only need the actual length.

## 5. Found while preparing this

- **Pulley grub screw hole.** `b_pulley40` in `tools/fusion_arm.py` cuts a 3.4 mm hole for an "M3 set screw (tap)",
  which is clearance size, so a screw won't hold in it. For now, open it to 4 mm and melt in an M3 insert. The hole
  sits between the shaft flat and the tooth roots, with room to spare. The CAD should change it to a 4.2 mm insert
  pocket at the next Fusion session (C2 and C34 share the builder).
- **Flange insert pockets broke into the 6810 journal** (fixed 26 Oct). The four M4 carrier inserts in the flange
  stem sat on R22 (`out_bolt_r`), but the stem is Ø50, so each Ø5.6 pocket came within 0.18 mm of the bearing
  surface; melting an insert in would have bulged it. `out_bolt_r` is now 19 for C2, which leaves 3.2 mm to the
  journal and 3.2 mm to the Ø26 bore. The flange and carrier were rebuilt in Fusion, the module's mate check passes,
  the STLs were re-exported and `c2/plate_3` and `plate_6` re-sliced. C34 (R18) was already fine. The docs/22 wall
  table had shown the 0.23 mm minimum without anyone following it up.
- **Pin lengths** (already in the `bom.csv` dowel row since the 20 Oct CAD count, repeated here because they matter
  now): the J2 output pins are 25 mm and the cover dowels 8 mm, so of the bought pins only the 25 ring pins fit as
  they are.
- **The motor plate is aluminium, 3 mm (D39).** `print_pack.py` writes `stl/bench/C2_motor_plate.dxf` and `.svg`
  (and C34's), 1:1 from the same numbers as the CAD. The SVG has a 50 mm scale bar: print it at 100%, check the
  bar, glue it to the sheet and use it as a drill and saw template. Or send the DXF to a cutting service. The motor
  slots allow ±3 mm for belt tension.
