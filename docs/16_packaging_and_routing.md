# 16: Packaging and Routing: where the electronics live, how the wires run, and how to model it

A plan, written 7 Oct 2026 before any CAD exists. It turns the electrical architecture in
[04](04_electronics_control.md) and the motor positions in [03](03_actuators_gearboxes.md) ("Hollow shafts and where
the motors go") into something you can draw in Fusion. Sizes marked *measure* are not confirmed; measure the real part
when it arrives and update this page.

> **Since then (26 Oct):** the CAD exists, generated by script ([19](19_cad_review.md) §14, [20](20_cad_full_arm.md)),
> with the electronics spaces drawn ([21](21_checks_and_review.md) §14). Two parts of this plan changed: the joint
> sensing in §3.7 became D42 (a rod on J2-J4, motor encoder + hall on J5/J6, [21](21_checks_and_review.md) §11), and
> the scope of the scripted Fusion work in §6 grew from parameters and masses to building every part. Both notes are
> marked where they apply.

## Short version

- **The harness is simple on purpose:** one 4-wire bus (24 V, GND, CAN-H, CAN-L) chained from driver to driver, every
  driver sitting on its own motor. What makes routing hard is not the wire count but three things: the **roll joints**
  (J1, J3, J5) twisting the bundle, the **wrist** (three motors, three drivers and a slip ring inside ~70 mm), and the
  **output encoders**, whose magnets want to sit exactly where the hollow shafts put the wires. How to read the
  joints anyway (and why only J2-J4 need an output encoder at first) is §3.
- **Model in Fusion, top-down:** a skeleton driven by the `arm.toml` dimensions, purchased parts as envelope bodies
  with connector and access keep-outs, each actuator as a module with fixed harness entry and exit points, and the
  harness as swept bodies along 3D sketch paths, checked for interference at the joint limits.
- **No need for SolidWorks.** Stay in Fusion. A Fusion API script (or one of the community Fusion MCP servers) is
  worth adding for two narrow jobs: pushing `arm.toml` numbers into Fusion parameters, and pulling masses and centres
  of mass back out. See §6.

**The driver sits on the back of every motor.** That adds roughly 20 mm to each motor's length *(measure the SERVO42D
with its connectors plugged in)* and puts a connector, a small OLED and three keys (Menu, Next, Enter) at the back.
The keys are only needed for setup, but the manual says to **calibrate each motor unloaded**, so design every
actuator so the **motor + driver comes out as a unit** (motor plate on 4 screws, belt slackened by a tensioner) without
taking the arm apart. Calibrate, set the CAN ID and bitrate on the bench, then install.

## 2. The harness

```
 base: bridge(120 Ω) ─ J1 drv ─┬─ (up through the turntable slot, J1 service loop)
                               └─ J2 drv ─ (J2 hollow input or loop) ─ J3 drv ─ (J3 hollow, twist section)
                                  ─ J4 drv ─ (J4 hollow) ─ J5 drv ─ (loop along forearm) ─ J6 drv
                                  ─ (loop across wrist pitch) ─ J7 drv ─ slip ring ─ pogo carrier (120 Ω) ─ tool MCU
 every segment: 24 V + GND (18 AWG silicone) and CAN-H/CAN-L (24 AWG twisted pair), daisy-chained in/out at each driver
```

**Termination.** 120 Ω at the base controller and at the **arm side of the tool changer** (the SERVO42D has a jumper
for its own terminator; leave all the others off). Terminating at the flange keeps the bus correct with or without a
tool; the tool board then carries no terminator.

**Current per segment** (worst case, all joints at full current and moving). Motor copper loss at rated current is
about 17 W per 60 mm LDO NEMA17, 18 W for the NEMA23, 9 W per 40 mm motor and 14 W for the pancake (its 7.2 Ω winding), plus up to ~22 W of
mechanical power per big joint ([03](03_actuators_gearboxes.md) §1b) and ~25 W for a tool. That gives:

| Segment | Carries | Supply current at 24 V |
|---|---|---|
| PSU → J1/J2 | everything | ~8-12 A peak ([04](04_electronics_control.md) sizes the whole arm at 250-300 W) |
| J2 → J3 onward | J3-J7 + tool | ~6 A |
| J5 → J7 | J6, J7 + tool | ~3 A |
| Through the slip ring | tool only (J7's driver is on the wrist side) | < 2 A (rated 4 A on 2 + 2 circuits) |

18 AWG silicone is fine everywhere; it is the bend and twist life that needs care, not the current. These are rough
upper bounds from the motor data in `arm.toml`; measure the real supply current on the bench.

**Connectors at every module boundary: XT30(2+2).** One plug carries 24 V, GND, CAN-H and CAN-L, so each actuator
connects to the next with a single connector and can be swapped as a unit. It is the connector Voron-style CAN
toolheads use. Each driver gets a short pigtail from its own header *(check the SERVO42D connector type on arrival)*
to an XT30(2+2) in and out.

**Twist at the roll joints.** J2, J4 and J6 are pitch joints: the bundle bends there, it doesn't twist, so a loop of
generous radius is enough. J1, J3 and J5 rotate the bundle about its own length:

| Joint | Range | Plan | To check |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | ±170° | Coiled or clock-spring loop under the turntable around the J1 motor, or a straight twist section up through the turntable | Length of the coil at both limits |
| J2 | ±120° | **External loop** beside the joint, which leaves the hollow input for the encoder rod (§3.5 M1) | Loop radius |
| J3 | ±170° | Twist section along the J3 axis: through a thin tube fixed to the output (§3.5 M2), then loose and straight behind the actuator, clamped only at the two ends | Free length needed (start with ~150 mm); the bundle fits an ~8 mm bore |
| J4 | ±120° (was ±140°, D40) | **External loop** (changed 12 Oct, was through the hollow input), so the axis takes the encoder rod (§3.5 M1) | Loop radius |
| J5 | ±180° | External loop along the forearm, or a twist section alongside the J5 planetary | Loop length at ±180° |
| J6 | ±120° | Loop across the wrist pitch axis | Clearance to the J7 motor |
| J7 | continuous | Slip ring | - |

There is no reliable rule for how much twist a loose bundle tolerates over a given length; treat 150 mm as a starting
assumption and **cycle a test harness 1,000 times through the full range on the bench** before freezing the design.
Use high-strand silicone wire for anything that moves, keep loop radii at 30 mm or more, and clamp each moving section
at both ends so the flexing happens where you designed it, not at a solder joint or a connector.

**CAN wiring.** Twist CAN-H and CAN-L together along the whole run (the MKS manual recommends shielded twisted pair
and a common ground). Daisy-chaining in and out of each driver means no stubs. Keep the twisted pair beside the power
pair, not wrapped around the motor phase wires.

**After an E-stop the drivers reboot.** The E-stop cuts the 24 V rail, and the SERVO42D/57D run their logic from that
rail too, so every driver restarts and loses its multi-turn count ([04](04_electronics_control.md)). It keeps the
angle *within* one motor turn, so a coarse joint reading is enough to recover the exact position without homing (§3.3).

## 3. Reading the joint angles on hollow joints

*Expanded 12 Oct 2026. The 7 Oct version of this section recommended a 1:1 belt pickup on J1-J4 and J7 and an on-axis
magnet "at the far end" of J5/J6. Working it through showed that neither holds as written (§3.6). This section
replaces it; the old text is in git history (commit `92f8656`).*

### 3.1 What a joint sensor is for

Three different jobs, and they need different sensors:

| Job | Why | How good it must be |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Know where the arm is after power-up** | The E-stop cuts the 24 V rail, the drivers reboot and forget their turn count (§2). Without help, every joint must sweep to a homing sensor | Only good enough to pick the right **motor turn** (§3.3): ±3.75° on J2-J4, wider elsewhere |
| **B. See what the gearbox does** | The motor encoder can't see backlash (~0.5-0.8°) or gear wind-up under gravity (~1-1.4° at the worst pose with the placeholder stiffnesses). Both move the tool by millimetres | ~0.05° at J2 for the 0.5 mm target (table below), which needs calibration |
| **C. Catch gross faults** | A belt that skips a tooth or a sheared pin makes motor and output disagree by a lot, and nothing else notices | Coarse (a few degrees) |

How far a joint error moves the tool, at the longest lever arm each joint has (J2 → TCP is 550 mm in `arm.toml` since
the 3-prong gripper moved the TCP to 110 mm past the flange on 20 Oct; this table used 520 mm before 26 Oct):

| Joint | Lever arm to the TCP | 0.1° at the joint moves the tool | One MT6701 count (360°/2¹⁴ = 0.022°) |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1, J2 | up to 550 mm | 0.96 mm | 0.21 mm |
| J3, J4 | up to 360 mm (elbow to TCP) | 0.63 mm | 0.14 mm |
| J5, J6 | up to 180 mm (wrist to TCP) | 0.31 mm | 0.07 mm |

**Accuracy and repeatability are different things.** A magnetic angle sensor's error (its integral non-linearity,
INL) is mostly systematic: the same angle reads wrong by the same amount every time. That doesn't hurt *repeatability*
(go back to a taught pose) at all. It hurts *accuracy* (go to a pose computed by the IK from a model). The 0.5 mm
target in [01](01_requirements.md) is repeatability, so job B needs a sensor that is repeatable and fine, and the INL
can be calibrated out later (§3.4).

### 3.2 What the motor encoder already gives

From the MKS SERVO42D/57D CAN manual **V1.0.9** (read 12 Oct, §5.1): the driver's encoder sits on the motor shaft and
reports **14 bits per motor turn** (`value`, 0-0x3FFF) plus a turn counter (`carry`), command `30H`. The single-turn
part is an absolute magnetic reading; only the turn counter starts again at power-up. The manual's "single-turn
power-on zero return" feature (§7.17.6) only makes sense if that is so. *Confirm on the bench:
power-cycle a driver, turn the shaft by hand, read `30H`.*

So after an E-stop the controller knows each motor's angle within one turn exactly, and has lost only *which* turn.
One motor turn is 360°/R at the joint:

| Joint | Ratio R | One motor turn at the joint | Coarse reading must be better than ±180°/R |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | 25 | 14.4° | ±7.2° |
| J2, J3, J4 | 48 | 7.5° | **±3.75°** |
| J5, J6 | 22.2 | 16.2° | ±8.1° |
| J7 | 15 | 24° | ±12° |

### 3.3 Coarse + fine: absolute joint angle from a cheap sensor

Any coarse reading of the joint angle that is better than ±180°/R picks the motor turn, and the motor encoder supplies
the fine part. With R the ratio (negative if the motor turns the other way), m the motor's single-turn reading in turns
(`value` / 16384), θc the coarse joint angle in degrees, and (θ₀, m₀) one pair recorded at calibration:

```
n = round( R·(θc − θ₀)/360 − (m − m₀) )        whole motor turns since the calibration pose
θ = θ₀ + 360·(n + m − m₀)/R                     the joint angle, as fine as the motor encoder
```

If the coarse reading is off by more than the window, θ comes out wrong by exactly one motor turn (7.5° on J2-J4), and
**nothing in the sum shows it**: the residual |θ − θc| still looks small. (Corrected 12 Oct: an earlier version of this
paragraph said the residual would catch it.) So the protection is the error budget below, plus a partial check:
`CanBackend` refuses a start whose residual lands in the outer third of the window (over ~2.5° on J2-J4), because a
coarse sensor that far off can't be trusted to be inside it. Math and the code: [14](14_math_guide.md) §16.

**Error budget for the coarse reading on J2** (the tightest case), uncorrected:

| Source | Size | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor INL | ≤ 1.5° | MT6701 ±1.5° max over −40…125 °C, ±1.0° typical at 25 °C aligned (datasheet v1.9, 2024; *15 Oct: was ≤ 1.1°, the MA732's figure, with the MT6701 at ±0.5° from the 2020 Rev 0.1*) |
| Mounting: magnet off-centre, rod or pickup run-out | ~0.3° | Estimate |
| Half the backlash | 0.25° | `arm.toml` |
| Gear wind-up at the worst static pose | 1.60° (16.8 N·m ÷ 600 N·m/rad, CAD masses), 0.66° with the spring | `arm.toml` placeholder stiffness |
| **Total** | **~3.65°** against ±3.75° | Very thin; subtract the model's predicted wind-up (τ/k, already in `deskarm.physics`) and it drops to ~2.05° (26 Oct; 3.5° and 2.1° with the estimated 14.9 N·m; 3.1° and 1.7° before the 15 Oct INL correction) |

J3/J4: 1.5 + 0.3 + 0.25 + 1.31 (6.9 N·m ÷ 300, CAD masses) ≈ 3.4° against ±3.75° (3.3° with the estimates, 2.9° before
15 Oct). What the code does (26 Oct): `CanBackend` takes the coarse reading from the *calibrated* encoder, so the INL is
already removed and about 1.9° is left on J2 (wind-up plus half the backlash), inside its 2.5° refusal limit (two
thirds of the window). It does not subtract the predicted wind-up yet: with an uncalibrated sensor J2 would land near
3.65° and the start would be refused. Adding `deflection` (as `DualLoop` computes it) to the coarse reading is a
small open item. J5/J6: about 3.2° against ±8.1°. So a
coarse sensor with a degree or so of error is enough for job A on every joint, including one read through a pickup
with some backlash.

**Joints that need no coarse sensor at all.** J1 (yaw) and J7 (tool roll) carry almost no gravity torque, so they don't
move while the power is off unless someone pushes them. The host saves the last joint angles; at power-up the saved
angle is the coarse reading. A hall sensor on each checks it at the first pass, and if the arm was moved by hand the
joint homes by sweeping, which is safe on a yaw axis and on a continuous roll. *13 Oct:* the hall switch wires to
that joint's own MKS driver, input IN_1 (the "home" input), and the host reads it with command 34H, so it adds no wire
through the joints. `deskarm.startup` and `CanBackend` do the check and the sweep (sim only; the band's position comes
from the CAD, its width and the switch's polarity from the bench). J2-J6 can sag or roll under gravity when
unpowered ([04](04_electronics_control.md)), so they need a real coarse sensor to skip homing.

### 3.4 The sensors

| Sensor | Resolution | Accuracy (datasheet) | Magnet | Off-axis? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MT6701** (MagnTek; 2 bought for the bench) | 14 bit over SSI/I²C (0.022°) | INL ±1.0° typical (25 °C, no misalignment), ±1.5° max over −40…125 °C (datasheet v1.9, 2024.05; the 2020 Rev 0.1 said ±0.5° typical) | Ø8 × 2.5 mm diametric NdFeB, 30-80 mT at the chip, gap ≤ 2 mm, centred within 0.3 mm | Not described in the datasheet |
| **MA732** (MPS MagAlpha) | 14 bit over SPI | INL 0.7° at 25 °C, 1.1° over −40…125 °C | 40-60 mT recommended | **Yes, "side-shaft":** chip beside a ring magnet. The raw error is a double sine of up to ~40° depending on geometry; a trim register (BCT) cancels it. A multi-pole ring gives several readings per turn |
| AS5600 (ams) | 12 bit (0.088°) | Not checked | Diametric disc | Not checked |

Two consequences:

- **For job A any of them is enough**; for job B the MT6701 or MA732 (14 bit) is the minimum, and only after
  calibration. Calibration: drive the joint slowly through its range in both directions and log the output sensor
  against `motor angle / R`. The difference is the sensor's INL (slow: repeats once per joint turn) plus the
  gearbox's own transmission error (fast: repeats once per motor or cycloid turn) plus backlash (flips with direction)
  plus wind-up (follows the gravity torque). Fit the slow part as a few harmonics of the joint angle: that is the
  correction table. *Code since 14 Oct:* `deskarm.encoders` (math in [14](14_math_guide.md) §18) fits the harmonics,
  the lost motion and the wind-up together. Wind-up under gravity is itself a sine of the joint angle, so a sweep at a
  single pose can't tell it from the sensor's first harmonic: sweep at two poses with different loads.
- **Large diametric ring magnets aren't stock parts.** A side-shaft reading around a hollow output needs a
  single-pole-pair (diametric) ring whose bore clears the harness (≥ 11 mm). The catalogues searched on 12 Oct list
  diametric rings with ¼-inch-class bores (K&J Magnetics) and nothing at 25 × 15 mm. Small diametric discs (Ø6-8 mm)
  are everywhere. So designs that put a disc on a small shaft are the safe ones.

### 3.5 Ways to get a magnet in front of a sensor on a hollow joint

The geometry problem: an on-axis sensor must sit on the **stationary** side (link A) on the joint's axis, looking at a
magnet that turns with the **output** (link B). On a joint with the harness through the bore, both ends of the axis
are taken: the back end by the input shaft (24× faster than the output on J2-J4) and the harness, the front end by
link B.

| # | Mechanism | How | Works when | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Through-rod** | A Ø6 mm rod fixed to the output flange runs back through the hollow input shaft (Ø12 on J3/J4; J2's bore is set in its CAD) to the back of the actuator, carried by an **MR126 bearing (6 × 12 × 4) inside the input bore**. A Ø6-8 mm diametric magnet on its end faces an MT6701 on the housing. Sketch below | The harness does *not* go through the bore (it loops outside). Pitch joints J2, J4: a loop is easy at ±120° | A rod, an MR126, a magnet. No backlash of its own: the rod carries no load |
| M2 | **Through-tube + small pickup** | A thin tube (≤ 11 mm OD, ~8 mm bore) fixed to the output runs back through the input shaft with the harness inside it. At the back, two **equal small GT2 pulleys** (e.g. 2 × 36T, ~23 mm) drive a stub with the magnet | The harness must pass through (J3, the roll joint with the long twist section) | Tube, 2 pulleys, short belt, stub bearings. The belt's backlash only matters for job B |
| M3 | Side-shaft | MA732 beside a diametric ring on an output hub, BCT trimmed | A ring of the right size can be bought (§3.4) | Custom board C (MA732 is a bare chip). Sourcing risk |
| M4 | Passive end of a fork | If a pitch joint's output is carried on both sides (a fork/yoke), the side without the gearbox has a free axis end: plain on-axis MT6701 | J2, J4 or J6 if the CAD makes them forks | A second output bearing; stiffer, but wider |
| M5 | **No output sensor** | Motor encoder + saved angle + hall check (§3.3) | Joints with no gravity torque: J1, J7 | One hall sensor |

```

   ┌──────────────┐
   │ board C      │  gap 1-2 mm
```

### 3.6 What the 7 Oct plan got wrong

1. **A 1:1 pickup needs two pulleys of the same size.** On J1-J4 the output is 60-100 mm across, so the sensor
   pulley would be as big as the joint. A smaller sensor pulley turns faster than the joint (say 3:1), and then the
   sensor is absolute only within 120° of joint travel, which loses job A. M2 avoids this by taking the 1:1 drive
   from a small tube instead of the big output.
2. **"On-axis at the far end" of J5/J6 has no stationary part to hold the sensor.** With the motor coaxial at the back
   and link B bolted to the output at the front, nothing stationary reaches the output's axis. J5/J6 need M3, M4 (J6
   as a fork) or a small geared pickup, and the 65 mm wrist housing decides which fits. That needs the wrist CAD.

### 3.7 The plan (recommended 12 Oct, accepted by Josh 13 Oct as D33; superseded 20 Oct by D42)

*20 Oct (D42, [21](21_checks_and_review.md) §11): the CAD uses the M1 through-rod on all three of J2, J3 and J4
(J3's harness loops outside like the others, so the tube and 36T pair below are dropped), and no output encoder on
J5/J6 (motor encoder + hall; a pickup can be added). Hall switches on all seven joints. The table below is the 13 Oct
plan, kept for the reasoning.*

| Joint | Scheme | Coarse (job A) | Fine (job B) | Harness change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **M5** | Saved angle, hall check | Not needed: no gravity torque, so no wind-up to see | None |
| J2 | **M1 through-rod** | MT6701 | MT6701, calibrated | External loop (already allowed in §2) |
| J3 | **M2 through-tube + 36T pair** | MT6701 | MT6701, calibrated (belt backlash in the calibration) | Through the tube instead of loose in the bore |
| J4 | **M1 through-rod** | MT6701 | MT6701, calibrated | **Change:** external loop instead of through the hollow input |
| J5, J6 | Decide in the wrist CAD: M4 for J6 if it is a fork, else a small geared pickup or M3 | Hall + homing sweep until then | Later | - |
| J7 | **M5** | Saved angle, hall check (homes by spinning) | Not needed | None |

That is **three output encoders on the arm at first (J2-J4)** plus two later for the wrist, and **hall sensors on J1, J7
and, until their encoders exist, J5/J6**. It fits the BOM rows as they stand (2 MT6701 bought for the bench, 5 more
in the 11.11 window, hall sensors in 11.11). Small extra parts if accepted: diametric magnets Ø6-8 × 2.5 mm (check
whether the modules include one), MR126 bearings, Ø6 mm rod, and for J3 a thin tube, two 36T GT2 pulleys and a short
belt. *Not yet in `bom.csv`: Josh asked to wait on the BOM until the 11.11 order is placed.*

**Parts (accepted with D33).** Every scheme above puts the magnet on the axis, so the side-shaft MA732 isn't
needed. **MT6701 everywhere**: 14 bit, two already bought, and the AS5600 costs about the same for a quarter of the
resolution (0.088°, ~0.8 mm at the tool through J2). Read it over **SSI** (3 wires, the full 14 bits, no I²C address
clash); check which pins the AliExpress modules break out. **Magnet Ø8 × 2.5 mm diametric NdFeB** (the datasheet's
recommendation) in a cap on the rod end, ~1 mm from the chip. **Board C = ESP32-C3 + MT6701 + SN65HVD230 + buck**: the
same Arduino toolchain and TWAI CAN as board B; an STM32G0 is smaller and cheaper but adds a toolchain. **Hall
switches** (A3144-type modules with the 6 × 3 mm magnets already in the BOM) on J1 and J7: they only have to confirm the
saved angle to ±7°. Fallback if time runs short: hall homing on every joint and no output encoders yet, but keep the
MR126 seats and rod holes in the CAD so the encoders drop in later.

**Try it on the bench first.** Give the J2 bench gearbox a Ø12 hollow input with an MR126 seat and put the bench
MT6701 on a through-rod. The bench then measures backlash and stiffness (its original job) *and* tests M1 and the
coarse + fine maths before the arm depends on them.

### 3.8 How board C talks to the host

The MKS drivers cannot read a second encoder, and running extra signal wires through every joint breaks the 4-wire
rule. So make **board C a CAN node**: MT6701 + a small MCU (an ESP32-C3 or an
STM32G0) + a CAN transceiver, powered from the bus through a buck regulator. That adds one node per joint (IDs 0x11-0x17,
say), which the bus load estimate in [04](04_electronics_control.md) has to include: about 2 more small frames per
joint per cycle. With the §3.7 plan that is three nodes at first (J2-J4), not seven. The host, not board C, does the
coarse + fine sum of §3.3: board C only reports its raw angle. This is a change to board C in
[10](10_drivers_and_custom_pcbs.md); decide it before drawing board C.

**The frame is defined (13 Oct, D34).** ID `0x10` + the joint's driver id (J2 = `0x12`), 4 bytes: the 14-bit angle
(big-endian), a flags byte (MT6701 status, CRC ok, no sensor) and a sequence number. The base controller already sends
it for the bench MT6701 (GPIO10/12/13), so board C's firmware is that code on an ESP32-C3, with the frame put on the bus
instead of the USB link. Layout and CRC caveat: [firmware/base_controller/README.md](../firmware/base_controller/README.md).

## 4. How to model it in Fusion

Work top-down so a change in `arm.toml` moves everything:

1. **Skeleton component.** *Done 13 Oct by [`tools/fusion_skeleton.py`](../tools/fusion_skeleton.py).* User parameters
   `base_height` 130 (209 since D36, 20 Oct: re-run the script or edit the parameter), `upper_arm` 190, `forearm` 180, `wrist_flange` 70, `tcp_offset` 80, `spring_anchor` 100,
   `spring_attach` 150 (mm) and `J1_min` … `J7_max` (deg); a "Skeleton" component with level planes at the shoulder,
   elbow, wrist centre, flange and TCP, construction points there, the seven joint axes (J1/J3/J5/J7 along +Z,
   J2/J4/J6 along +Y, matching the DH frames so positive angles agree with the software) and a stick-figure sketch.
   Changing a parameter moves everything (checked). Fusion joints with the limit parameters come when the actuator
   modules exist. Everything else references the skeleton, never absolute coordinates.
2. **Purchased-part envelopes first.** Simple bodies for each motor (42.3 square; 60 / 39 / 20.5 mm long; NEMA23 57
   square × 76.5), each driver (42 × 42 or 57 × 57 × *measured* depth), bearings (6810: 50 × 65 × 7; 6806: 30 × 42 ×
   7), slip ring, PSU, Pi. Give each envelope two extra bodies: a **connector keep-out** (the plug plus ~15 mm for the
   wire to turn) and an **access keep-out** (the screwdriver or hex key path). Swap in vendor STEP files later
   (StepperOnline and Mean Well publish them; check MKS for the drivers) without moving anything.
3. **One component per actuator module** (J1, J2, J3/J4, J5/J6, J7), inserted from the skeleton. Each module defines
   its interface: the bolt circle it mounts on, the harness entry and exit points, and where its XT30(2+2) sits.
   J3/J4 and J5/J6 are one design inserted twice, so the interface has to suit both places.
4. **Harness as bodies.** For each segment, a 3D sketch path from connector to connector through the joint, swept
   with a ~6 mm circle (the 4-wire bundle). Do it for each roll joint in two configurations (both limits) and measure
   the path length both times: the difference is the loop or twist length you must leave.
5. **Check at the limits.** Drive the Fusion joints to their limits (a motion study, or just a few saved positions)
   and run *Interference* on the harness bodies against the housings. Most packaging mistakes show up at J5 = ±180°
   with J6 = ±120°.
6. **Boards from the mechanics, not the other way round.** Draw each board outline, its mounting holes and connector
   positions in Fusion where the board has to live, export the outline as DXF into KiCad's Edge.Cuts layer, lay out
   the board, then bring the populated board back as a STEP from KiCad. Kairo can take over the schematic step once it
   generates schematics; layout stays manual until its Phase 3.
7. **Base board layout** can start now, in 2D: PSU along the back edge, E-stop at the front edge, boards A/B and the Pi
   between them, cable route to the J1 slot. It needs no other CAD to exist.

**Masses back into the software** (done 20 Oct, [20](20_cad_full_arm.md) §9, `tools/cad_masses.py`). The `[[masses]]` list in `arm.toml` is where CAD has to report to. Two cautions:
- Fusion computes mass from the material density, but printed parts are not solid. Use an effective density for
  printed PETG instead of PETG's 1.27 g/cm³. This plan guessed ~0.8 g/cm³; the slicer later gave ~0.9 for the housings
  and links ([21](21_checks_and_review.md) §3), and `cad_masses.py` uses the slicer's grams. Correct it from the first
  part you weigh.
- Read each lump's mass and centre of mass with the arm in the zero pose (straight up), in the base frame, because
  that is the convention `arm.toml` uses.

## 5. Per-joint packaging notes

| Joint | Packaging | Harness | Encoder | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | NEMA17 + 42D coaxial under the turntable, 25:1 cycloid, 6810 output bearings | Rises beside the motor through a slot in the turntable, coil around the motor | None: motor encoder + saved angle + hall (§3.3) | The coil must not rub on the J1 motor at ±170° |
| J2 | NEMA23 + 57D on the turntable beside the axis, 2:1 HTD-3M 15 mm belt, 24:1 cycloid | External loop | Through-rod + MT6701 at the back | Spring anchor and cable path stay clear of the harness |
| J3 | 60 mm motor + 42D beside the axis, 2:1 belt, cycloid, hollow | Twist section through an output tube | Through-tube + 36T GT2 pair + MT6701 | The longest twist section in the arm |
| J4 | Same module as J3; motor ~70 mm back (231 mm belt; the housing and motor do not fit closer), or ~80 mm with a 252 mm belt (option C) | External loop | Through-rod + MT6701 | Belt length if the motor moves back |
| J5 | 40 mm motor + 42D coaxial, 2-stage planetary 22.2:1 (output stage 18 mm face) | Loop along the forearm | Hall first; encoder scheme from the wrist CAD | ±180° loop length |
| J6 | Same module as J5, near the wrist (or at the elbow end with a belt, option C) | Loop across the pitch axis | Hall first; on-axis if J6 is a fork (§3.5 M4) | Clearance to J7 at ±120° |
| J7 | Pancake + 42D beside the axis, 3:1 GT2 belt, ~5:1 planetary, hollow for the slip ring | Slip ring | None: motor encoder + saved angle + hall | Length: three motors and a slip ring within ~70 mm. The tool should sit close to the wrist |

## 6. Would connecting a CAD program to the coding agent help?

| Option | What it gives you | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fusion, by hand** (current plan) | Everything in §4; free with an education licence; all the docs assume it | - | **Keep** |
| **Fusion + API script or MCP server** | The agent can read and set user parameters, list components with mass and centre of mass, export STL/STEP, and run the interference check. **Since 13 Oct an Autodesk Fusion MCP connection is available** (Fusion running, scripts through its Python API); it built the skeleton. Community servers also exist: [Faust Machines](https://github.com/faust-machines/fusion360-mcp-server), [prim-design](https://github.com/prim-design/fusion-mcp), [AuraFriday](https://github.com/AuraFriday/Fusion-360-MCP-Server) | - | **In use.** The 7 Oct verdict was "narrowly: parameters in, masses out, not for drawing the housings". From 20 Oct, at Josh's request, every part was generated by script through this connection ([20](20_cad_full_arm.md)) |
| SolidWorks | Stronger assembly tools; a dedicated cable-routing add-in exists in the premium editions (check what a student licence includes) | A second CAD to learn mid-project; Windows only; licence | **No.** Four wires don't need a routing add-in, and the schedule risk is mechanical design time |
| SolidWorks + MCP | Same as the Fusion MCP | Same as above | No, for the same reason |

So: no SolidWorks. Parameters in is `tools/fusion_skeleton.py` (13 Oct); masses out is `tools/cad_masses.py` with
`fusion_run.py sweep bodies` (20 Oct), which closes the loop between CAD and the torque budget.
